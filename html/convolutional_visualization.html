Understanding Convolution

The word Convolution sounds really big and complicated, but it is actually just a fancy way of playing a matching game. Let’s break it down so it makes perfect sense using The Detective Analogy.

1. The Input (Sandbox)

The big grid of numbers representing shapes. It comes from your picture.

The input size depends entirely on what you are looking at. If you are looking at a tiny icon, your sandbox might be small (like 10x10). If you are looking at a giant, high-quality photograph of a cat, your sandbox will be massive (like 1000x1000)!

2. The Kernel (Magnifying Glass)

The pattern you are searching for. It is chosen by you.

The programmer (you!) gets to choose how big the magnifying glass is. A small glass (like 2x2 or 3x3) is great for finding tiny details, like the tip of a cat's whisker. A bigger glass (like 5x5) is better for finding larger shapes, like the whole cat ear.

3. The Feature Map (Notepad)

Where you write the "Match Score". Its size is calculated!

It is never random. Because your magnifying glass takes up space, it can't slide as far in a small sandbox. There is a simple math rule to figure out the size of the Feature Map:

Input Size - Kernel Size + 1 = Feature Map Size

Why do computers do this?

If a computer is trying to recognize a picture of a cat, it uses thousands of these little magnifying glasses. Some look for pointy ears, some look for whiskers, and some look for tails. By sliding all these magnifying glasses over the picture, the computer builds a "treasure map" of where all the cat parts are, helping it guess that the picture is indeed a cat!

Are they always those sizes?

That is a brilliant question! You are thinking exactly like a computer scientist. The short answer is no, they are not always those sizes! They change all the time, and they are never random. There is a strict rule (a calculation) that decides the size of the Feature Map.
